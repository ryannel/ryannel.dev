The early 2000s were a good time to be a curious kid on the internet. A time of dial up modems,
secret forums, peer to peer networks and pirated software. Getting online still felt like and
occasion and the internet was young and messy.

I got hooked on software security, on hacking. I used to trawl the forums downloading
compressed bundles of guides, software and pirated books, reading everything I could get my
hands on. Those bundles were a mess of unorganised files, and mixed in alongside The Hacker
Manifesto, guides on port scanning and security were other topics like stories of phreaking
(hacking telephone networks) from the 70s, the Anarchists Cookbook, and strangely enough lock
picking.

Those early days started off a quiet and life long interest in locks.

I find them facinating and as AI models became better and better at Blender and 3D modeling,
one of the first things I wanted to try and make were some 3D models of locks.

## Get the models

The [lock project on GitHub](https://github.com/ryannel/experiments/tree/main/lock_mechanisms) has the editable Blender models, the scripts that build them and the finished animations. I’ve included two skills in `.agents/skills/` you can use to build a lock of your own and check how its parts fit and move. To download just this experiment, run

```sh
git clone --filter=blob:none --sparse https://github.com/ryannel/experiments.git
cd experiments
git sparse-checkout set lock_mechanisms
cd lock_mechanisms
```
