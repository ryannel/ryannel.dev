I built a small coaching tool for myself — somewhere to think a decision through before making it.
It was useful in a way I didn’t expect, and awkward in a way I probably should have.

The quality of the advice tracked almost exactly with how much detail I gave it. Generic input,
generic output. It only became useful once the input included the specifics — constraints,
history, the things that never make it into a written summary.

Which is the problem. The context that made it work was exactly the context I’d want to keep on
my own machine.

<Callout type="hypothesis">
  For this tool, and I suspect for others like it, the binding constraint wasn’t capability or
  cost. It was that the context which made it useful was private, and the architecture had to earn
  the right to see it.
</Callout>

That pushed me toward questions I’d been treating as someone else’s problem: what runs locally,
what gets abstracted before it goes anywhere, and what I’m willing to lose in quality to keep the
sensitive parts at home.

I don’t have a settled answer. What I took from it is that "just send everything to the frontier
model" stopped being a reasonable default for me the moment the tool became genuinely useful.
