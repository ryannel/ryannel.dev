I kept using these three words for everything, which made my own design notes circular. The split
I’ve settled on for now is about *who decides*:

| | Decides what to do | Decides how to do it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tool | caller | tool |
| Skill | caller | caller, guided |
| Agent | itself | itself |

A tool is a capability with a contract. A skill is accumulated judgement about *how* to approach
something — knowledge that would otherwise live in someone’s head, made reusable. An agent is the
thing that decides.

## Why I’ve found the distinction useful

Mostly because it tells me where to put a fix. When output is wrong, I can ask:

1. was the capability missing? → tool;
2. was the approach wrong? → skill;
3. was the decision wrong? → agent, or the context it decided from.

<Callout type="update" label="Update — August 2026">
  I’ve stopped treating this as three categories and started treating it as a spectrum of how much
  judgement is delegated. The boundaries blurred constantly once I was building rather than
  diagramming, and the question that earned its keep was "how much is this thing deciding?" rather
  than "which of the three is it?".
</Callout>
