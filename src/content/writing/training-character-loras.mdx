I spent a few weeks training character LoRAs, mostly out of curiosity. It turned into the most
useful thing I did that quarter, for reasons that had little to do with images.

Getting a consistent character turned out to be mostly an evaluation problem. I couldn’t tell
whether a run was better by looking at one output. I needed a fixed set of prompts, the same
seeds, and the discipline to compare like with like — otherwise I was just reacting to whichever
sample happened to look good.

<Callout type="surprise">
  Dataset composition mattered far more than training configuration. Twenty well-chosen images beat
  two hundred mediocre ones, and beat almost any amount of hyperparameter fiddling on the same bad
  set.
</Callout>

The part I’ve carried into other work: when the output is subjective, I make no progress until I
fix what I’m comparing against before I start changing things. That has held for the prompts,
agents and retrieval strategies I’ve tried it on since, though I came to it from faces.

One more, which cost me a week: pushing likeness hard enough cost me the ability to put the
character in new situations. I suspect something similar shows up whenever I tune hard on a narrow
objective, but I’ve only actually watched it happen here.
