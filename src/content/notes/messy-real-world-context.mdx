Every time I watched an agent fail on one of these systems, the postmortem landed in the same
place. The model could reason. It just didn’t have the right things in front of it, and nothing in
the system knew that.

## The failures didn’t announce themselves

A reasoning failure looks like a reasoning failure. A context failure looked like a confident,
plausible, wrong answer — which was harder to catch, because the output had the same shape as a
good one.

<Callout type="surprise">
  The runs that worried me most weren’t the ones that failed loudly. They were the ones that
  produced something reasonable from a partial picture and gave no signal that the picture was
  partial.
</Callout>

## What happened when I widened the context window

My first reflex was to give it more. That helped up to a point, and then it stopped helping in ways
I didn’t notice for a while:

- retrieval that was *topically* relevant but structurally irrelevant;
- three versions of the same fact, one of them stale;
- everything the system knew, and nothing about what mattered.

What moved the needle for me was treating context assembly as its own component with its own
contract — something I could test:

```python
# The unit under test isn't the model. It's what reaches the model.
def test_migration_context_includes_owning_service():
    ctx = assemble(task="rename column", entity="orders.customer_ref")
    assert ctx.includes_schema_owner()
    assert ctx.excludes_unrelated_services()
```

Once context assembly was a testable thing, my questions got sharper. Not "is the model good
enough", but "did the right five facts reach it, and how would I know".

> The interesting work turned out not to be the prompt. It was everything that decided what the
> prompt got to say.

<Callout type="hypothesis">
  In the systems I’ve worked on, the limit has been context engineering rather than model
  capability — and it behaved like a systems problem, not a prompting one. That’s a narrow slice of
  systems, and I don’t know yet how far it carries.
</Callout>
