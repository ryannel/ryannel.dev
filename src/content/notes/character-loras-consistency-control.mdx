I spent a few weeks training character LoRAs, mostly out of curiosity. It turned into the most
useful thing I did that quarter, for reasons unrelated to images.

Getting a consistent character is an evaluation problem wearing a costume. You can’t tell whether a
run is better by looking at one output. You need a fixed set of prompts, the same seeds, and the
discipline to compare like with like — otherwise you’re just reacting to whichever sample happened
to look good.

<Callout type="surprise">
  Dataset composition mattered far more than training configuration. Twenty well-chosen images beat
  two hundred mediocre ones, and beat almost any amount of hyperparameter fiddling on the same bad
  set.
</Callout>

The transferable lesson: when the output is subjective, the only way to make progress is to fix
what you compare against before you start changing things. That applies to prompts, agents and
retrieval strategies at least as much as it applies to faces.

Also — and this one cost me a week — control and fidelity trade off against each other. Push
likeness hard enough and the model loses the ability to put the character in new situations. I
suspect there’s a version of that trade-off in every system tuned hard on a narrow objective.
